# Does the market's climbing favourite pay? (F.7, 7 Oct 2026)

Hassan, 6 Oct: strategies "decide pre, and during peak for each unique city's
peak ... and the market movement where the likely winner starts climbing
rapidly". Before building that trigger, `tools/f7/momentum_study.py` tested it
on the venue's own record. Its rules were committed (`2cd1f58`, 08:21Z) before
it was first run. Output: `data/eval/f7/momentum_2026-10-07.json` (sha256
`59d731d8...`; two runs give the same bytes, 32 s). Research only.

**Result: no rule is adopted.** Buying the leading bucket after it climbs 0.10
or 0.20 in an hour, before or during the city's peak, lost money on average
after the spread and the fee in all twelve rules, and so did buying the
favourite with no condition. At hourly resolution the market prices its
climbing favourite about right: during the peak, a 0.20 climb wins 68.8% at an
average price of 68.8 cents. So the momentum trigger is not built; the live
strategies keep deciding at each city's own peak-relative checkpoints.

## What was tested

- **Data:** 7,957 closed events of the 48 active cities, 30 Dec 2025 - 31 Aug
  2026, hourly YES prices stamped before 1 Sep (the sealed test starts then).
- **Instants:** each whole local hour 09:00-18:00. PRE is 3 h to 0 h before the
  city-month's peak hour, DURING is the peak to 1 h after.
- **The leader:** the highest-priced bucket (price at most 2 h old). **The
  climb:** its price change over the hour before.
- **Twelve rules** (0.10 or 0.20 climb, PRE or DURING), one share at the first
  instant of the phase where the rule fires:
  - **M:** the market alone;
  - **A:** and the forecasts agree (status-v1: seven models' lead-1 span under
    4.8 C);
  - **W:** and the leader is the bias-corrected forecast's own bucket.
- **Cost:** the first price after the instant (never the signal's own price)
  plus the median half-spread of the archived books before 1 Sep, plus the
  taker fee 0.05 x p x (1 - p).
- **Adopted only if** 100 trades on 30 dates, the 99.58% date-bootstrap interval
  (Bonferroni over 12) wholly above zero, and still positive at the
  75th-percentile spread.

- Before the peak, a climb says less than its price: M 0.10 and A 0.10 lose
  with the interval wholly below zero.
- The forecast's agreement (A) or its own bucket (W) raises the hit rate, and
  the price rises with it; none turns positive.
- Per city (descriptive, not a selection): with at least 20 trades, 18 of 47
  cities were positive on M 0.10 DURING and 13 of 47 on M 0.10 PRE, about what
  noise around a small loss gives.

## Limits, said plainly

- The record's hourly price is quoted, not executable. On post-peak rows the
  books' real ask sat 7.7 cents above it on average
  (`docs/S10_AFTER_COSTS_2026-09-28.md`), so these costs are if anything low,
  which would make every rule worse, not better.
- The books before 1 Sep (23-31 Aug) give the spread; above a price of 0.7
  they hold only 33 quotes. The archived real asks cover 11 trades at most:
  too few to judge.
- Hourly prices cannot see a climb inside the hour. The minute-level study
  (`docs/MARKET_REACTION_2026-09-28.md`) found the market reprices a new
  reading within minutes, and buying after it lost money. A faster trigger
  than the hourly tick is not indicated by either study.
- The peak hours were computed in September from observation history; they
  only label instants PRE or DURING.

## What F.7 does instead

The rest of F.7 stands on its own evidence: the status gate (only Candidate
cities, from the measured disagreement rule) and a per-city list learned
within Rule 11 bounds, both in shadow.
